Experience Thelonious Monk beyond the familiar studio albums.

LIVE FROM NEW YORK

Thelonious Monk Quarter- Vol. 1 & 2

New York · 1958 & 1963

Two remarkable live recordings capture the Thelonious Monk Quartet at two legendary New York jazz venues: he Five Spot in 1958 and Birdland in 1963.

The performances feature Charlie Rouse, Ahmed Abdul-Malik, Roy Haynes, Butch Warren, and Frankie Dunlop.

Hear performances of Monk classics including:

  • Blue Monk
  • Rhythm-a-Ning
  • Epistrophy
  • Light Blue
  • Off Minor
  • Friday the Thirteenth
  • Light Blue
  • I'm Getting Sentimental Over You
  • Straight, No Chaser
  • Evidence

LIVE AROUND THE WORLD

LIVE IN STOCKHOLM

Volume 1 & 2

May 16, 1961

Monk's quartet—featuring Charlie Rouse, John Ore, and Frankie Dunlop—takes the stage in Stockholm during Monk's international touring years.

Across two volumes, the group performs selections including “Jackie-ing,” “'Round Midnight,” “Bemsha Swing,” “Blue Monk,” “Rhythm-a-Ning,” and “Epistrophy.”

The collection also includes previously unreleased bonus material.

Explore Stockholm

LIVE IN PARIS

Volume 1 & 2

February 24, 1964

Captured in Paris during one of Monk's most celebrated periods, this performance features Charlie Rouse on tenor saxophone, Butch Warren on bass, and Ben Riley on drums.

The two-volume set includes extended performances of Monk favorites such as “Four in One,” “Straight, No Chaser,” “Well, You Needn't,” “Blue Monk,” “Hackensack,” “Rhythm-a-Ning,” and “Bright Mississippi.”

Explore Paris

LIVE IN PARIS

Volume 3

November 3,1967

A larger ensemble joins Monk for this 1967 Paris performance, bringing together musicians including Phil Woods, Johnny Griffin, Clark Terry, Charlie Rouse, Ben Riley, and Larry Gales.

The program includes performances of “Ruby, My Dear,” “We See,” “Epistrophy,” “Evidence,” and “Blue Monk.”

WHY THESE RECORDINGS MATTER

Music Beyond the Studio

Experience Thelonious Monk beyond the familiar studio albums.

These recordings preserve moments that might otherwise have disappeared: club performances, international concerts, private recordings, and previously unheard material.

Thelonious Records was established in 2001 in part to preserve and officially release rare Monk material. The label describes its archive as including privately recorded performances and other material that had never previously received an official release.

Each recording offers another perspective on Monk: as a composer, bandleader, and performer constantly reshaping his music from one performance to the next.
